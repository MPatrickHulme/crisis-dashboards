Reading the figure. Left axis: Congressional Sentiment Score — the mean per-lawmaker position on U.S. military force (−0.5 oppose … +0.5 support), 3-model ensemble (Grok + Luna + Gemini), relevant statements only, weeks with ≥3 speaking members, each member weighted equally within the period Periods carrying fewer than that many speakers are not estimated; the line is interpolated across them so the trend reads continuously, and the shaded band shows the distance from zero — above the dashed line Congress was on balance for the use of force, below it against; solid = tripartite, dashed = 8-item instrument. Right axis: share voting to support U.S. force on the era's use-of-force roll calls — for authorization measures this is the share voting Yea; for withdrawal / funds-cutoff measures it is the share voting Nay (each tooltip states what Yea meant). Both axes read support-up and CSS 0 aligns with the 50% line, so votes and sentiment are directly comparable. Large purple markers = whole chamber (diamond = Senate, square = House); small red/blue diamonds on the same stem = Republican and Democratic shares; a gold halo marks measures that carried. Hover any marker for the bill, tally and party split with links to the sources (voteview covers every Congress; congress.gov where coverage exists); click a marker to open the primary source. Gold dotted line: Enduring Freedom begins. Bottom: weekly statement volume by party (blue D, red R, gray other/unknown).
