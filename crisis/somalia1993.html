Reading the figure. Left axis: the Congressional Sentiment Score, defined as the mean per-lawmaker position on the use of U.S. military force (−0.5 oppose to +0.5 support), estimated by a three-model ensemble (Grok, Luna, Gemini) over statements coded as relevant, months with ≥3 speaking members, each member weighted equally within the period Periods below that threshold are not estimated; values are interpolated between adjacent estimates so that the series is continuous, and the shaded band reports the distance from zero, above which the chamber was on balance supportive of the use of force and below which it was opposed; the solid line reports the tripartite instrument and the dashed line the 8-item instrument. Right axis: the share voting to support the use of force on contemporaneous roll calls. For authorizing measures this is the share voting Yea; for withdrawal and funds-cutoff measures it is the share voting Nay, so that both axes read support-upward and CSS 0 coincides with the 50% line, rendering sentiment and voting directly comparable. Each tooltip states the meaning of a Yea on that measure. Large purple markers denote the full chamber (diamond, Senate; square, House); the small red and blue diamonds on the same stem denote the Republican and Democratic shares; a gold halo indicates a measure that carried. Hovering a marker reports the bill, tally and party split with links to sources (Voteview for all Congresses; congress.gov where coverage exists); clicking opens the primary source. Gold dotted line: Task Force Ranger deploys. Lower panel: monthly statement volume by party (blue, Democratic; red, Republican; gray, other or unknown).
